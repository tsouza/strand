## Primary license

`Unicode-3.0`): "use, copy, modify, merge, publish, distribute, and/or sell copies of
the Data Files or Software" is permitted without restriction, on the sole condition
that the copyright and permission notice accompany the materials. No copyleft, no
share-alike, no field-of-use restriction beyond a trademark clause. This is the
license covering ICU's code and the large majority of its bundled data, including —
confirmed by its total absence from the file's per-component third-party notices,

the 568-line LICENSE file, meaning Thai's word-segmentation data carries no separate
third-party attribution and is covered directly by the primary Unicode-3.0 grant.

## Bearing on STRAND's Apache-2.0-compatibility policy (`CLAUDE.md` §1)

Every word-break dictionary ICU bundles for the scripts invariant 6 names — Chinese,
Japanese (via `cjdict.txt`), Lao (via `laodict.txt`), and Thai (covered directly by
the primary Unicode-3.0 grant, no separate notice) — carries a permissive,
non-copyleft license: BSD-style or the primary Unicode License V3 itself. None
carries a GPL/LGPL or share-alike condition. This is a materially different outcome
from CC-CEDICT's CC BY-SA 4.0 terms (`references/cc-cedict-and-lindera-cc-cedict-license.md`),
which does impose a share-alike condition incompatible with vendoring under
Apache-2.0 without relicensing STRAND's own derived conformance vectors.

Unicode-3.0 itself is not a license this project has previously determined
Apache-2.0-compatible (the existing precedent set for MIT — tantivy, FAISS,
`cwida/FastLanes`, `CLAUDE.md` §1 — does not by itself cover a different license
family). Unicode-3.0 is OSI-approved and FSF-listed as a free-software license
(permissive, non-copyleft, notice-preservation only); this RFC amendment is the
first place this project makes that determination explicitly, on the same standard
already applied to MIT: permissive terms, no copyleft obligation, compatible with
combination into an Apache-2.0-licensed work provided the notice is retained.
